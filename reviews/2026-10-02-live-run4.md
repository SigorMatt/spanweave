### A1. The "declaration floor" is a ceiling, and the sentence drawn from it is false

`SPEC.md:2412-2417`, and propagated verbatim to `CHANGELOG.md:513-517`,
`TASKS.md:12865-12866`, `tests/live_cost.py:107-111`, `WORKPLAN.md:584-586`,
and the bodies of `7ddd630`, `60a831b` and `0718ba8`.

Two sub-agents working from different briefs, with no shared context, reached
this independently; I then re-derived it myself.

SPEC says, of the echo shape:

> turns 1–100 declare 24.6 receipts per record and turns 301–400 declare
> 174.8, so **7.1× is the floor** of that rise for any implementation that
> builds each declared relation once … The measured rise is 5.43×, *below*
> the floor, so the per-record cost is **sublinear in the declarations the
> record carries**; the difference is the fixed cost of classifying and
> absorbing a span at all. A number under 7.1× is the good outcome, and a
> target under it could only be met by suppressing relations the telemetry
> stated.

The paragraph refutes itself in three steps: a floor cannot be undercut, the
measurement undercuts it, and the clause explaining why *is* the reason it is
not a floor. With per-record cost `F + c·d` (`F` = fixed work, `c` = cost per
declaration, `d` = declarations):

```
ratio = (F + c·d₂)/(F + c·d₁) ≤ d₂/d₁ = 7.096,  equality only at F = 0
```

so 7.1× bounds the rise **from above**. From SPEC's own printed stretch
figures (0.3784 and 2.0567 ms/record): `c ≈ 11.18 µs/declaration`,
`F ≈ 0.103 ms/record`, measured ratio 5.435 — below 7.1× precisely because
`F > 0`.

The derived figures themselves are all correct and reproduce exactly:
`C(100,2)/201 = 24.63`, `(C(400,2) − C(300,2))/200 = 174.75`, ratio `7.096`,
and `§4.2.1` (`SPEC.md:1192-1197`) does promise `n(n−1)/2` with none
suppressed. The arithmetic is right; the word attached to it is wrong, and the
conclusion built on the word is false.

**The false sentence is `SPEC.md:2416`**: "a target under it could only be met
by suppressing relations the telemetry stated." Reaching 1.5× needs
`c ≈ 0.374 µs` — a ~30× cheaper edge build, with **no declaration dropped**.
Worse, the ratio has no lower bound at all: raising `F` (being uniformly
slower) drives it toward 1. So the criterion L17 was judged against is
*gameable*, not unmeetable — the real defect in it is stronger and more
interesting than the one SPEC states.

This matters beyond wording. `60a831b` is the commit whose stated purpose was
"cost stated as promises … no bare ratios," and §10.6 is the public cost
contract. The one sentence the batch existed to make recomputable is the one
that does not survive recomputation. The honest form is roughly: *7.1× is the
rise in the work the input declares; ms/record can only rise by less, and the
gap is fixed per-record cost. 1.5× was unreachable for this implementation at
~11 µs/edge, which is an empirical claim about constants, not a consequence of
§4.2.1.*

Note for whoever fixes it: `7ddd630`'s own body already contains the correct
argument, and it is the strongest sentence in the range — "'`Edge.__init__`
linear in receipts' and 'ms/record flat across the stream' cannot both hold
unless building an edge were free." That rests on edge-build cost, not on a
floor. The floor framing added afterwards is weaker than the sentence it was
meant to support.
